## Overview

Population aging is a major challenge facing societies worldwide in the 21st century, and the cognitive abilities of older adults warrant particular attention. Focusing on older adults in Shanghai, this study used a picture-naming task to examine the relationship between cognitive decline and language-related cognitive abilities. Participants frequently produced semantic-related errors, visual errors, circumlocutions, and hyponym responses. Naming latency was strongly associated with variables such as familiarity and age of acquisition. The findings suggest two tendencies among older adults: they attend more to overall shape while overlooking visual details, and retrieve familiar objects more frequently and quickly. In contrast to findings reported for Indo-European and other languages, this study identified affix-omission errors but no phonological errors. These results suggest that the mechanisms of cognitive aging are related to the type and structure of linguistic information, supporting the Specific Information Theory.
