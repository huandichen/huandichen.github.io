## Overview

Population aging is a major challenge facing societies worldwide in the 21st century, and the cognitive abilities of older adults warrant particular attention. **Focusing on older adults in Shanghai, this study used a picture-naming task to examine the relationship between cognitive decline and language-related cognitive abilities.**

Participants frequently produced semantic-related errors, visual errors, circumlocutions, and hyponym responses. Naming latency was strongly associated with variables such as familiarity and age of acquisition. The findings suggest two tendencies among older adults: they attend more to overall shape while overlooking visual details, and retrieve familiar objects more frequently and quickly. In contrast to findings reported for Indo-European and other languages, this study identified affix-omission errors but no phonological errors. These results suggest that the mechanisms of cognitive aging are related to the type and structure of linguistic information.

## Reflection

My undergraduate training in Chinese Language and Literature gave me a strong foundation in theoretical linguistics. I later came to appreciate how *broadly* linguistics connects with other fields, and my interest in human behavior led me to explore the intersection of linguistics and psychology. Using the well-established and accessible picture-naming task, I began asking how language and cognition interact. 

Looking back, I see this project as **the starting point** for many of my later interests. I’m grateful to have had the opportunity, and I remember the experience fondly.
